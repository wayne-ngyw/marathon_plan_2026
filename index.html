Race-Day Pacing Was Genuinely Disciplined
Second-by-second data from the May 3 ASICS Kumamon 10K shows laps 2–10 all landing within 4:49–4:56/km — remarkably even for an early-season race. Heart rate climbed steadily from 160 to 191 bpm across the same steady pace, which is classic cardiac drift from fatigue and heat, not a pacing mistake. ✦ Strength
Four Years of Data Show Real Cardiovascular Gains
Full-history FIT analysis (2022–2026, 326 runs) shows average heart rate per run dropping steadily even as volume and intensity climbed: 169.5bpm (2023) → 168.6bpm (2024) → 156.2bpm (2025) → 151.0bpm (2026) — an 18.5bpm drop from 2023 to now. Running meaningfully more and harder for meaningfully less cardiac cost is about as clean a fitness-improvement signal as this data can show. (2022 sits at 156.4bpm but on only 7 runs — too small a sample to read much into.)
Avg HR per run: 169.5bpm (2023) → 151.0bpm (2026), −18.5bpm despite far higher training load
✦ Strength
A Second, Independent Data Source Confirms the Same Trend
New this update: Apple Health and Google Health exports add resting heart rate and VO2max — collected by different sensors (Apple Watch through 2024, then Garmin/Fitbit Air from 2025) than the in-run HR analyzed above — and they tell the same story. Resting HR sat flat at 59–63bpm every year from 2020 through 2024, then dropped to 50.4bpm in 2025 and 49.6bpm in 2026 so far — a genuine ~13bpm improvement, not device-switch noise (Google Health's independently-synced 2026 monthly averages land within 1–3bpm of Apple Health's every month, Mar–Sep). Device-estimated VO2max follows the same dip-and-recover shape: 44.3 (2022) → 38.3 (2024, the low point) → 44.3 (2026) — the training buildup didn't just lower cardiac cost during runs, it improved resting cardiovascular fitness and aerobic capacity too.
Resting HR: 62.7bpm (2024) → 49.6bpm (2026) · VO2max: 38.3 (2024 low) → 44.3 (2026), two independent sources agree
⚠ Watch Out
Some Interval-Day HR Readings Look Like Sensor Noise, Not Fitness
A handful of RMAC/PACE sessions show HR spiking to 201–211bpm — above your validated true max of 191bpm from the May 3 race. These aren't single-second blips; they're sustained for minutes, but they only ever show up during hard interval reps with heavy arm-swing, a known failure mode for wrist-based optical HR sensors (motion cross-talk gets misread as pulse). The clean, smooth 160→191bpm climb during the continuous-effort May 3 race is the more trustworthy max. Zones are left unchanged, but treat any single-session HR data from RMAC/PACE days with a grain of salt — a chest strap would fix this if precise interval HR ever matters for your training decisions.
3 sessions with 200bpm+ readings, all during RMAC/PACE — true validated max stays 191bpm

Real Easy Pace Is Slower Than the Plan Assumed — Now Quantified
Comparing Jan–Apr long runs to May–Sep long runs at the same HR effort (roughly 145–162bpm both periods) shows a consistent ~54 sec/km slowdown in the hot season: ~7:11/km average in the cooler months vs. ~8:05/km in the hot/humid months — a ~13% pace penalty for identical aerobic cost. This confirms it's heat/humidity-driven aerobic decoupling, not a fitness regression — the discipline holding HR steady is the strength here. Recommendation: stop chasing the plan's printed easy pace number on hot days and run by heart rate (cap ~150 bpm) instead; expect ~45–55 sec/km to come back "for free" as autumn cools things down, which is worth building into how you read October/November paces.
Jan–Apr easy pace: ~7:11/km · May–Sep easy pace: ~8:05/km · same HR band both periods
◉ The Numbers
Race Shoe Pick: Sky Tokyo Over Edge Tokyo
Garmin's Running Dynamics data (ground contact time ~278ms, vertical ratio ~9.25%, true cadence ~156–165 steps/min) all sit in the moderate-to-fair band, not the quick-turnover range — biomechanically a stride runner, not a cadence runner. ASICS splits its Metaspeed racing line exactly along that line: Edge Tokyo is built for cadence-style runners, Sky Tokyo for stride-style runners who speed up by lengthening their stride. No dedicated racing flat is in the current gear rotation (Boston 12, Novablast 5, EVO SL, Megablast, Pegasus 40, UltraBoost, Trabuco 14), and there's still time before Fuji (Dec 13) to buy and break one in across 2-3 tune-up races. Try both on in person, but the data points to Sky Tokyo as the better starting fit.
GCT 278ms · vertical ratio 9.25% · cadence ~160spm → stride-runner profile → Sky Tokyo
